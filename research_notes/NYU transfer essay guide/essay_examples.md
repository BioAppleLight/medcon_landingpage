# Published Examples of Successful NYU Transfer and "Why NYU" Essays

Research date: 2026-10-25. About 18 search/fetch calls. Overall finding: **published, verified-admit NYU *transfer* essays are very rare online.** Most published "NYU essays that worked" are first-year "Why NYU" supplements from the 400-word era (about 2017-2022), and NYU has since discontinued that first-year prompt. The transfer statement (2,500 characters) still asks a "why transfer + why NYU" question, so those Why NYU examples are still the best available models for the "how NYU supports my goals" half.

Note on quotes: all quotes below come through a page-summarizer tool. They are short, but check them against the source before publishing them verbatim.

## Q1. Where are published examples, and what does each contain?

### Takeaway
Only about 3 NYU *transfer* examples are publicly findable: CollegeEssayGuy's Chapman-to-Steinhardt MCC essay, a Stern internal-transfer case study (no text published), and an unverified 2016 Gallatin forum draft. None of the three has an independently verified admit. Strong "Why NYU" examples with commentary exist on CollegeAdvisor (stated as admitted), CollegeVine (admit status not stated), AdmitSee (self-reported admits, Class of 2019) and PrepScholar (the author's own admitted CAS essay). I found no usable Reddit, Medium or YouTube essay texts, and no Shemmassian or Prompt NYU transfer examples.

### Cited Findings

**Current transfer prompt context**
- The NYU transfer prompt reads: "Please provide a statement that addresses your reason(s) for seeking transfer and the objectives you hope to achieve," plus how NYU and the chosen school or program can support those goals. The limit is 2,500 characters. — [Koppelman Group, How to Transfer to NYU 2026](https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2025/12/21/how-to-transfer-to-nyu-2026)
- Fall 2024 transfer: more than 10,000 applications and 2,243 admits, about 22%, against 9.2% for first-years. — [Koppelman Group 2026](https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2025/12/21/how-to-transfer-to-nyu-2026). A lower-credibility aggregator gives 21.9% (1,489 of 6,800) for "Fall 2025-2026". The applicant counts conflict, so treat that source as unreliable. — [TransferGoat](https://transfergoat.com/transfer-acceptance-rates/nyu/)
- NYU discontinued the first-year "Why NYU" supplement for 2024-25. It was replaced by a 250-word "bridge builder" essay, which is still the first-year prompt in 2026-27. — [PrepScholar](https://blog.prepscholar.com/why-nyu-essay); [CollegeEssayGuy NYU supplemental guide](https://www.collegeessayguy.com/blog/new-york-university-nyu-supplemental-essay)
- A 2021 guide describes two transfer prompts: a "Why Us" prompt (2,500 characters) and a "reasons for transferring and objectives" prompt (3,000 characters). This is the older format. — [PenningPapers (2021)](https://penningpapers.com/2021/10/06/the-ultimate-guide-to-writing-the-nyu-transfer-essays/)

**Transfer Example A: Chapman (Dodge College, PR & Advertising) to NYU Steinhardt, Media, Culture & Communication (CollegeEssayGuy)**
- Credibility: published with annotations by Ethan Sawyer. Admission is implied but not explicitly stated, and the year is not given. — [CollegeEssayGuy transfer essay examples](https://www.collegeessayguy.com/blog/college-transfer-essay-examples)
- Structure (about 8 paragraphs): creative outlets across several family moves; why the student chose Chapman; the major and internship mismatch; activities at Chapman; NYU; career dream and internship plan.
- Reason for leaving: the internships the student wanted required living locally and working at least 15 hours a week, and the program "did not offer any fashion courses."
- NYU resources named: only Steinhardt, the MCC program and a communications path that combines fashion and marketing. The essay names no courses, professors or NYC specifics, so it is thin on NYU detail.
- Key line: "Now, I'm ready to move onto the next phase of my education studying Media, Culture, and Communication at NYU Steinhardt."
- Sawyer's annotations: "Show growth and trajectory" (magazine covers to a jewelry brand); "It's not you, it's me... well, it's kinda you, too" (name the mismatch while showing effort at the current school); "Show what you bring to the new relationship."
- CollegeEssayGuy's general 7-step transfer structure: (1) open with core values through a concrete image; (2) briefly say why you chose your current school; (3) give specific reasons for leaving without bashing the school; (4) show how you made the best of it; (5) state a dream or goal, even a provisional one; (6) explain how the new school helps, with researched courses, faculty and resources; (7) close briefly with a memorable line. — [CollegeEssayGuy](https://www.collegeessayguy.com/blog/college-transfer-essay-examples)

**Transfer Example B: NYU CAS to Stern internal transfer, "Angel" (The Admissions Essay Guru, posted Jan 20, 2025)**
- Credibility: a consultant's client testimonial that says she was accepted. The essay text is not published. — [Admissions Essay Guru](https://theadmissionsguru.com/blog/accepted-to-nyu-stern-transfer-essay)
- Prompt: three mini-questions totaling 900 words (the internal-transfer format). The essay took seven drafts.
- Moves: opens in the present tense mid-story; weaves the three answers into one narrative "well-rounded rather than fragmented"; explains the major change "without being negative"; uses "showing not telling with concrete details"; cuts lines that do not build momentum. Her global background and social-impact leadership were framed as fit for Stern.

**Transfer Example C: São Paulo art-school acting student to NYU Gallatin (EssayForum, March 2016)**
- Credibility: low. This is an unverified draft and no outcome is reported. — [EssayForum](https://essayforum.com/undergraduate/nyu-gallatin-international-transfer-67916/)
- Reason for leaving: the current school offers no undergraduate playwriting, directing or producing classes, and its training felt individualistic and mainstream. The student wants ensemble and devised theater.
- Key line: "transferring to a program in which I can design my own major"; Why NYU: "I yearn to create or join a theater company that produces new work."
- Reviewer feedback: "try to avoid catch phrases"; the paragraphs are too dense; it should say more about what the student would contribute to Gallatin.

**Transfer excerpt (strong vs. weak), PenningPapers 2021 (consultant-written illustrations, not verified admits)**
- Weak: "NYU contains all the resources I would need to be properly prepared for a career in business and finance." This is generic because nearly every applicant could write it.
- Strong: an AI-startup goal tied to a moral purpose: "To create a bottom-up based AI startup would be the start of a fantastic business journey for me." — [PenningPapers](https://penningpapers.com/2021/10/06/the-ultimate-guide-to-writing-the-nyu-transfer-essays/)

**"Why NYU" Example 1: Stern, actuarial science (CollegeAdvisor)**
- Credibility: the page says the authors "were admitted to New York University." Names are redacted, no year is given, and it was the 400-word prompt. — [CollegeAdvisor NYU essay examples](https://www.collegeadvisor.com/essay-guides/nyu-essay-examples-and-why-they-worked/)
- Opening: "I always had a keen interest in numbers, probability, and finance."
- NYU resources named, by course number: "STAT-UB 21 'Introduction to Stochastic Processes' and STAT-UB 15 'Statistical Inference and Regression Analysis'"; the International Business Exchange Program.
- Former admissions officer's commentary: "extremely compelling"; the student's interests line up with their background and with what NYU offers; the essay shows conviction and lets the reader picture the passion "in the classroom."

**"Why NYU" Example 2: public health policy (CollegeAdvisor)**
- Opening: "Before I began interning for the International Rescue Committee's refugee youth acclimation program". This is an anecdote about a refugee youth named Mahdi.
- NYU resources named: CASSR research on racial bias and inequality; the courses "Social Policy in Modern Societies" and "Race and Ethnicity"; a Washington Square Park scene ("Clad in lavender shirts, NYU students were camped out in the center of the park").
- Commentary: the essay moves from the student's search for answers, through Mahdi's story, to NYU as the answer, and shows a community-oriented, purposeful student. — [CollegeAdvisor](https://www.collegeadvisor.com/essay-guides/nyu-essay-examples-and-why-they-worked/)

**"Why NYU" Examples 3-6 (CollegeVine, Nov 2022; admit status not stated)** — [CollegeVine, 4 Great Why NYU Essay Examples](https://blog.collegevine.com/nyu-essay-example)
- *Stern luxury marketing:* opens with "My mother never takes off her Cartier necklace that my father gave her 10 years ago on their anniversary." It names the luxury marketing track, "The Dynamics of the Fashion Industry", "Brand Strategy & Planning", IBEP and LARA (Luxury and Retail Association). Praised for not mentioning NYU in the hook and for "more is less," focusing on three opportunities. Critique: throwaway details lack emotional insight.
- *Nutrition & Food Studies:* opens with a quote from an NYU info-session panelist. It names adjunct Kayleen St. John and the courses Food History, Food Photography and Food Systems. Praised for a *purposeful* use of NYC, its food scene rather than the city in general. Critique: needs more reflection on her heritage conflict.
- *Computer Science:* opens with "Hacking represents my ideal college experience." It names the CS Colloquium, CILVR Lab, Center for Genomics and Systems Biology, the undergraduate thesis and the NYUAD Hackathon for Social Good. Praised for a unifying hackathon metaphor and short paragraphs. Critique: tells rather than shows.
- *Steinhardt Food Studies:* names "Essentials of Cuisine: International", Food Production Management, Club EAT and the Urban Farm Lab. Critique: does not explain what drives the interest, and mixes topics within paragraphs. CollegeVine recommends one aspect of NYU per paragraph.

**"Why NYU" Examples 7-9 (AdmitSee, NYU Class of 2019; self-reported admits on student profiles, not verified)** — [AdmitSee](https://www.admitsee.com/blog/how-to-tackle-the-why-nyu-essay-prompt-with-examples)
- *Sociology:* names the department speaker series and the seminars "Should We Worry about inequality?" and "Cultural Durability and Social Change", and treats NYC as a fieldwork site: "it also provides a place to conduct fieldwork that not many schools can match - namely, New York City."
- *Love-letter metaphor:* opens with "I've been in love with you for quite some time now." It names cross-school minors and closes on what the student will contribute. A risky but distinct voice.
- *Stern swimmer:* opens with "Are you going to eat all that?" AdmitSee itself notes the NYU connection is thin, since generic alumni praise could apply anywhere. This is a useful near-weak contrast.

**"Why NYU" Example 10: PrepScholar author's own admitted CAS essay (undecided student)**
- Names Cantonese study alongside Mandarin, NYU Shanghai study away, a creative writing reading series, early music ensembles and Steinhardt electronic composition facilities. The author admits it is "not necessarily the best piece of writing." — [PrepScholar](https://blog.prepscholar.com/why-nyu-essay)

**Not useful / not transfer**
- CollegeEssayAdvisors' "NYU admissions essay examples" (Santería, Friday Night Concerts and others) are personal statements that name no NYU resources and give no individual admit confirmation. — [CollegeEssayAdvisors](https://www.collegeessayadvisors.com/essay-examples/nyu-admissions-essay-examples/)
- I could not read the collegeessay.org "NYU Transfer Essay Examples" page because its content would not load. Search snippets suggest a "gap at current school, NYU closes the gap" framing. — [collegeessay.org](https://collegeessay.org/blog/how-to-write-transfer-essay/nyu-transfer-essay-examples)
- NYU News published a first-person essay by a community-college (College of the Canyons) transfer who had been rejected as a first-year. It is a story about the journey, not the application essay. — [NYU News, 2024](https://nyunews.com/culture/2024/04/04/nyu-graduation-personal-essay/)

### Inferences
- Because so few verified NYU transfer essays exist, a guide should label the CollegeEssayGuy Steinhardt essay as the only full NYU transfer model, and borrow the "specific NYU resources" layer from the Why NYU examples.
- Search showed YouTube titles such as "how i got into nyu with a low gpa | my stats, essay, & tips!" and "Yourselfie NYU (Transfer Edition)", but only through scraped aggregator pages, so I could not verify them.

### Gaps
- I found no Reddit (r/TransferStudents, r/nyu) threads with full admitted transfer essays. Search tools did not surface Reddit content.
- I found no Shemmassian, Prompt or Admissionado NYU transfer essay examples. Shemmassian only has first-year bridge-builder material.
- I found no examples from the 2023-2026 transfer cycles with essay text.

## Q2. What patterns recur across successful essays, and what distinguishes them from weak ones?

### Takeaway
Successful essays (1) open with a concrete, personal origin moment and do not mention NYU in the hook; (2) give a brief, academic, non-negative reason for leaving the current school, tied to a specific missing resource; (3) name 2-4 specific NYU resources (courses, by number where possible, labs, faculty, programs) and connect each to a stated goal; (4) use NYC only purposefully, as a field site or industry access; and (5) close briefly on a future goal and what the student will contribute. Weak essays are generic ("NYU has all the resources"), praise the city or prestige, list resources without connecting them, or complain about the current school.

### Cited Findings
- Hook: a strong opening often does not mention NYU, which builds intrigue. — [CollegeVine](https://blog.collegevine.com/nyu-essay-example). Start with a small, zoomed-in origin story about your academic interest. — [Koppelman 2026](https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2025/12/21/how-to-transfer-to-nyu-2026)
- Reason for leaving: keep it to at most one paragraph and focus on academics; "Transferring just to move to a more competitive school is not a compelling pitch"; leave out complaints about the social scene. — [Koppelman 2026](https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2025/12/21/how-to-transfer-to-nyu-2026). Explain the mismatch while showing what you did at your current school ("it's kinda you, too"). — [CollegeEssayGuy](https://www.collegeessayguy.com/blog/college-transfer-essay-examples). Explain the change "without being negative." — [Admissions Essay Guru](https://theadmissionsguru.com/blog/accepted-to-nyu-stern-transfer-essay)
- Show action, not just ambition: explain why you cannot pursue the goal where you are. — [Koppelman 2026](https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2025/12/21/how-to-transfer-to-nyu-2026). A common mistake is not explaining why transferring is *necessary*. — [PenningPapers](https://penningpapers.com/2021/10/06/the-ultimate-guide-to-writing-the-nyu-transfer-essays/)
- Specificity without a catalog: the supplement "shouldn't become a 'best of' list" — [Koppelman](https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2025/12/21/how-to-transfer-to-nyu-2026). "More is less": three opportunities, one aspect per paragraph — [CollegeVine](https://blog.collegevine.com/nyu-essay-example). Course numbers and named centers (STAT-UB 21, CASSR) drew "extremely compelling" praise — [CollegeAdvisor](https://www.collegeadvisor.com/essay-guides/nyu-essay-examples-and-why-they-worked/)
- NYC: "What you absolutely should avoid is gushing about NYU's location"; tie the city to an NYU-specific opportunity — [PrepScholar](https://blog.prepscholar.com/why-nyu-essay). A purposeful use of NYC (the food scene for food studies, fieldwork for sociology) was praised — [CollegeVine](https://blog.collegevine.com/nyu-essay-example); [AdmitSee](https://www.admitsee.com/blog/how-to-tackle-the-why-nyu-essay-prompt-with-examples)
- Fit and conviction: the student's interests should line up with their background *and* NYU's offerings — [CollegeAdvisor](https://www.collegeadvisor.com/essay-guides/nyu-essay-examples-and-why-they-worked/)
- Contribution: show what you bring — [CollegeEssayGuy](https://www.collegeessayguy.com/blog/college-transfer-essay-examples); the Gallatin reviewer flagged a missing contribution — [EssayForum](https://essayforum.com/undergraduate/nyu-gallatin-international-transfer-67916/)
- Closing: describe goals for the five years after graduation in three sentences or fewer — [Koppelman 2026](https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2025/12/21/how-to-transfer-to-nyu-2026)
- Weak markers: generic resource claims (PenningPapers' weak example); alumni or prestige praise that could apply anywhere (AdmitSee's Stern swimmer essay); campus buildings or coffee shops; vague careers — [PenningPapers](https://penningpapers.com/2021/10/06/the-ultimate-guide-to-writing-the-nyu-transfer-essays/); [AdmitSee](https://www.admitsee.com/blog/how-to-tackle-the-why-nyu-essay-prompt-with-examples). "I want a bigger school" reads as weak — [TransferGoat](https://transfergoat.com/transfer-acceptance-rates/nyu/). Telling rather than showing (CollegeVine's CS critique).
- Strategy: declare the intended CAS major and do not plan to switch later; Stern is extremely competitive, with most transfer spots going to internal NYU transfers; NYU Abu Dhabi and NYU Shanghai take no transfers — [Koppelman 2026](https://www.koppelmangroup.com/blog/2025/12/21/how-to-transfer-to-nyu-2026)

### Inferences
- For 2,500 characters (about 400 words), a workable skeleton is: hook/origin (about 15%), current-school growth plus the specific gap (about 25%), 2-3 NYU resources each tied to a goal (about 45%), future plus contribution close (about 15%). This is my synthesis of the CollegeEssayGuy and Koppelman guidance, not a sourced formula.
- The verified-admit examples (CollegeAdvisor) differ from the weak one (AdmitSee's Stern swimmer) mainly in resource specificity, not in prose quality.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative or controlled evidence linking these patterns to admit outcomes. All of it is counselor opinion plus a small number of examples.

## Q3. What do counselors say in commentary and annotations on these examples?

### Takeaway
The commentary consistently praises fit between background and resources, a focused selection of resources, purposeful use of NYC, and a non-negative transfer rationale. It consistently criticizes telling instead of showing, underdeveloped details and generic praise of NYU.

### Cited Findings
- CollegeAdvisor (a former admissions officer): the Stern essay is "extremely compelling" because the interests line up with the student's background and with NYU, and it conveys conviction; the public health essay arc runs search for answers, then Mahdi, then NYU as the answer. — [CollegeAdvisor](https://www.collegeadvisor.com/essay-guides/nyu-essay-examples-and-why-they-worked/)
- CollegeVine: "more is less"; one NYU aspect per paragraph; elaborate throwaway details emotionally; show rather than tell. — [CollegeVine](https://blog.collegevine.com/nyu-essay-example)
- Ethan Sawyer: show growth and trajectory; balance "it's not you, it's me"; show what you bring. — [CollegeEssayGuy](https://www.collegeessayguy.com/blog/college-transfer-essay-examples)
- Yelena Shuster: open in the present tense mid-story; integrate multi-part answers; cut lines that do not build momentum; seven drafts. — [Admissions Essay Guru](https://theadmissionsguru.com/blog/accepted-to-nyu-stern-transfer-essay)
- PenningPapers: the "HYPMS exercise" (imagine you were admitted to Harvard, Yale, Princeton, MIT and Stanford, then explain why you would still choose NYU) to find genuine NYU-specific reasons; state short- and long-term objectives. — [PenningPapers](https://penningpapers.com/2021/10/06/the-ultimate-guide-to-writing-the-nyu-transfer-essays/)

### Inferences
- The commentary converges across independent consultancies, which strengthens confidence in the patterns even though no source has outcome data.

### Gaps
- No official NYU admissions-office annotations of transfer essays were found. NYU publishes no "essays that worked" page, unlike Johns Hopkins.
